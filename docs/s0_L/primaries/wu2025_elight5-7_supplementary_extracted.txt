S1. The synchronization dilemma in recurrent operation
To illustrate the synchronization dilemma in a recurrent operation, consider the iterative formula y[n]=x[n]+y[n–1], with its physical implementation depicted in Fig. S1(a). Ideally, the delay is τ0, but due to synchronization or fabrication errors, the actual delay becomes 1.1τ0. Assuming the input signal is a periodic sequence and y[0]=0, the ideal output would mirror the periodicity of the input. However, the real output signal increasingly diverges into chaos as iterations progress, revealing the cumulative effects of temporal misalignment. Generally, the maximum temporal misalignment should remain much smaller than τ0/s, where s is the input sequence length. For a system operating at 1 GHz with sequence lengths in the hundreds, achieving the required sub-picosecond control accuracy is a significant challenge for current synchronization techniques. Additionally, we simulate a common scenario where the optical recurrent system is bandwidth-limited. S5. Wavelength routing and asynchronous wavelength cycling based on MRR
In the main text, two primary MRRs—small MRR and large MRR—are utilized to perform the wavelength routing function. The small MRR operates on a single wavelength, whereas the large MRR simultaneously handles two different wavelengths. Consequently, the free spectral range (FSR) of the small MRR must be at least twice that of the large MRR. In practice, the designed large and small MRRs are shown in Fig. S5(a) and Fig. S5(c), respectively. Their measured transmission spectra are presented in Fig. S5(e) and Fig. S5(f). The small MRR has an FSR of 3.6 nm, while the large MRR has an FSR of 8.14 nm, satisfying the basic requirement. In the experiment, eight wavelengths are employed, with a wavelength span of 0.9 nm, distributed evenly within the two FSRs of the large MRR. The dotted line marks the adjacent wavelength channels of the current resonant wavelength. Crosstalk between adjacent wavelength channels is measured at –15.5 dB for the large MRR and –23.8 dB for the small MRR, which is sufficiently low to ensure high-accuracy recurrent computing.
The MRM is adopted from the foundry's basic library and features a smaller radius with a larger FSR. Fig. S5(g) illustrates the transmission spectrum of the MRM and its spectral shift under an injection current of 0.1 mA. A significant blue shift is observed, indicating that the MRM achieves ultra-high modulation efficiency under forward bias. The fabricated WRU is shown in Fig. S5(h). To measure the WRU's latency, the experimental setup depicted in Fig. S5(i) was used. A signal pulse was passed through both a reference waveguide with a short length and the WRU. The WRU was powered by a continuous-wave supply light. The output signals from the reference waveguide and the WRU were captured by an oscilloscope, allowing the relative displacement and pulse broadening to be analyzed. The pulse broadening corresponds to the RC-related latency of the WRU.

Chinese word segmentation processing is a fundamental task in Chinese language processing. The accuracy of word segmentation significantly influences the accuracy of subsequent processing tasks, while the efficiency of word segmentation affects the real-time performance of subsequent. Chinese sentences, with Chinese characters as the most basic unit of expression, can be regarded as sequences based on individual Chinese characters, where the boundary division between words within the sentence is implicit. The purpose of Chinese word segmentation is to identify the boundaries between hidden words, that is, to label each Chinese character in the sentence with its corresponding word position information. This allows the labeled word sequence to reflect the original meaning of the Chinese sentence.
The process of segmenting Chinese sentences based on statistical models involves several steps. Firstly, parameters required for the learning model are estimated from the input dataset and feature library obtained through preprocessing. Subsequently, a convergent word segmentation model is trained. Then, utilizing the trained word segmentation model, the optimal word position tag sequence for unannotated sentences is predicted to identify the word boundaries within the sentence. Finally, post-segmentation processing algorithms are employed. Transformation rules are extracted based on common word segmentation errors in the training corpus of the word segmentation model. These rules are then used to annotate the predicted annotation set of the model. This aims to reduce annotation errors in the prediction results and improve the prediction accuracy of the word segmentation model.

As for the electrical feedback scheme, intermediate results of x(t) and Winu(t) should be detected, summed up in the electrical domain, through nonlinear activation, and reloaded to the optical network. Compared with the asynchronous scheme, it involves two more times of detection and one more time of modulation in each cycle. Therefore, we can write the energy consumption of the electrical feedback scheme as: 12.48 pJ×s×N×2+20 pJ×s×N×3+28.2 pJ×s×N×4 =1582.1 pJ (s=4, N=2), where the energy efficiency of FPGA is 28.2 pJ/OP and there are one addition and one nonlinear operation in the electrical domain. For the energy efficiency calculation of the chip-reuse technology for long sequence processing, we consider one time of on-chip computing and one time of electrical relay.
